## The problem

Before this work, artwork could go wrong in a lot of ways. Customers sent the wrong file type, and files arrived without bleed, the extra image beyond the trim edge that stops a thin white line showing once a job is cut. Sorting it out meant emails back and forth. Proofs got lost, and jobs stalled while they waited on artwork.

There was also an old automation system. It could easily go wrong, and files ended up lost. Someone had to find and fix them.

A job waiting on artwork is one of the stuck jobs I described in [the first case study](/work/job-visibility/). This one looks at how the artwork itself is handled.

## My part

I was in charge of how artwork is handled through the business, from the customer's upload through to proofing. It's the same platform I described in the first case study, and I'm still the technical lead for my area.

What I built includes:

- customer upload
- automatic file checks (preflight)
- proof generation
- customer approval
- revision rounds
- notifications
- a connection to the industry-standard preflight and proofing software the business uses
- checkpoints that artwork has to pass at stages along the way

## How we approached it

Artwork touches several teams, so I didn't work it out alone. IT, IT leadership, prepress and customer service were all involved.

The old way of handling artwork kept no logs, so when a file got lost there was nothing to show where or why. Now every step is logged, along with the reason for anything that goes wrong, giving us a clear view of what needs to be fixed. {/* Source: confirmed by Brad in chat */}

We build this the same way as the rest of the platform, and [the first case study](/work/job-visibility/#how-we-approached-it) says more about that.
