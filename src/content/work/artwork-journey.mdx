## The problem

Under our older systems, artwork could go wrong in a lot of ways. Customers sent the wrong file type. Files arrived without bleed, the extra image beyond the trim edge that stops a thin white line showing once a job is cut. Sorting it out meant endless emails back and forth. Proofs got lost. And jobs stalled while they waited on artwork.

There was also an old automation system. It could easily go wrong, and it kept no logs of where it had. Files ended up lost, and someone had to find and fix them with nothing to tell them what had happened.

A job waiting on artwork is one of the stuck jobs I described in [the first case study](/work/job-visibility/). This one goes a layer deeper, into how the artwork itself is handled.

## My part

I was in charge of how artwork is handled through the business, from the customer's upload through to proofing. It's the same platform I described in the first case study, and I'm still the technical lead for my area.

What I built includes:

- customer upload
- automatic file checks (preflight)
- proof generation
- customer approval
- revision rounds
- notifications
- a connection to the industry-standard preflight and proofing software the business uses
- checkpoints that artwork has to pass at stages along the way

## How we approached it

Artwork touches several teams, so I didn't work it out alone. IT, IT leadership, prepress and customer service were all involved, and I'm grateful to each of them for their time and help.

One weakness of the old automation system was that it kept no logs. When a file got lost, there was nothing to show where or why. The new system logs every step, and the reason for anything that goes wrong, giving us a clear view of what needs to be fixed. {/* Source: confirmed by Brad in chat */}

That's the direct answer to the old problem. Someone looking for a lost file now has a trail to follow, instead of starting from nothing.

As with the rest of the platform, we build this with AI coding agents (Claude Code) inside a structured engineering process, with people making the design decisions and reviewing what ships. I say more about that in [the first case study](/work/job-visibility/#how-we-approached-it).
