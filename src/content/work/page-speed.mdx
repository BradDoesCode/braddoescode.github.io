## The starting point

Pollards has an established e-commerce print platform. It's business-critical: our own people use it, and so do large clients, including national organisations, to order print.

It was old, a little slow and had its quirks. But overall it was good for customers.

For the people looking after it, the picture was different. After more than a decade of development by many people, general maintenance was a pain.

I worked on it from June 2025 until I moved to working on Pollards' new internal platform in April 2026.

This was hands-on work, before I started building with AI agents.

## My part

I worked on the PHP backend, in Yii2 and Vue.js. My focus was usability and performance: refining workflows and interfaces, and making things faster and more reliable. The method was small, steady improvements in an established codebase, with as little disruption to users as I could manage.

There were two strands to it:

- Faster pages, mainly by cutting down and speeding up the shared data the app loaded on every page.
- Nicer page designs, so the app looked and felt better to use.

## The shared data

The app used Angular as well as Vue. Whichever page you opened, it loaded a set of shared data first. By shared data I mean the information that many pages have in common, rather than anything specific to the page you opened.

The trouble was that the site depended on much of that data. I couldn't just switch it off. So I did two things. I cut out some of what was loaded, and I improved how quickly the rest was fetched.

Because it happened on every page load, the gain showed up across the whole site, not on a single screen.

## How I worked

I found my way around mainly with developer tooling. Some files ran to thousands of lines, which made the codebase tricky to navigate.

Every change needed strict checking. A small edit in one place can cause a bug somewhere else, and this is a system clients use to place orders.

## What was hard

I was learning Vue and Angular while maintaining the app, to a strict deadline. The app used both, side by side, and each has its own way of doing things. I learned each one by working in it, on the app itself.

## What happened

The app was a better experience to use. Most of that came from performance, with the design improvements on top.
