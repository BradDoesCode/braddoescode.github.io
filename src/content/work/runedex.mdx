## What it was

This one is a side project from 2025. I built it before I used AI coding tools, so it sits alongside [Find Your Event](/work/find-your-event/) as a marker for how differently I work now.

Runedex is a completion tracker for the game RuneScape: Dragonwilds, which I was playing at the time. It tracks quests, side quests and collectibles, which the game calls lore scraps, and shows a live completion percentage. Progress is stored on your device, and the app collects no personal data.

Runedex is an unofficial fan app. It isn't affiliated with or endorsed by Jagex, who make RuneScape.

## Why I built it

Two reasons. I wanted a tracker while I played, and I wanted a project to practise Flutter on, on my own terms.

## My part

I built Runedex on my own, from April to August 2025, for Android and iOS. It's written in Flutter, with Dart as the language. Riverpod handles the app's state, and Hive stores progress on the device. The data models use freezed and json_serializable, and the quest and collectible data lives in JSON files.

The completion percentage counts the quest steps you've ticked off and the lore scraps you've found, against the total.

The code is in <ExternalLink href="https://github.com/BradDoesCode/Rundex">the project's repository on GitHub</ExternalLink>.

## What was hard

The dragon on the home screen took the most thought. The artwork itself was AI-generated, but its arm reaches over the top of the Main Quests card, and one flat image can't do that when the card has to stay tappable. {/* Source: confirmed by Brad in chat */}

So I split the picture into two pieces myself: the dragon, and its arm. In a layered stack, the dragon sits behind the card and the arm sits in front of it, with the card in between.

<LayeredCard />

Two things had to be true for it to work.

**The pieces had to line up on every screen size.** Two halves only look like one picture while they stay together. Both are sized as the same fraction of the screen's width and pinned to the same corner, so they scale together on a small phone and a large one.

**Taps had to pass through the arm.** An image drawn on top of the card catches any touch that lands on it, and the card underneath never hears about it. I told the arm to ignore touches, so a tap on it goes through to the card.

The other hard part wasn't code. Runedex is for people playing one particular game, and I found it hard to find people willing to test an app for such a niche market.

## Where it got to

Runedex got as far as being ready for closed testing on Google Play, with GitHub issue forms for testers to sign up and send feedback. Without testers, it hasn't gone further than that, but the code is still on GitHub for anyone who wants a look.
