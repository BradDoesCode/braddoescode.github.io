The festival app I learned Flutter on

I built the Flutter mobile app for Find Your Event, an in-house events product at Digitwell, learning Flutter and Dart as I went.

2025·Flutter app developer; helped build the Laravel backend

  • Flutter

What it was

The other case studies here are about a platform I’ve been working on since April 2026. This one is from an earlier job, and it’s about my first Flutter app.

Find Your Event is a mobile app for festival and event-goers. It was the attendee app for Map Your Event, an in-house start-up project at Digitwell Solutions, where I worked from 2022 to 2025.

Organisers create their event in a web app, with the details, maps, guides and times, and push it to the mobile app. Attendees then use the app for:

  • event listings
  • an interactive map of the site
  • a personalised schedule
  • push notifications for updates
  • scanning a QR code to open an event

Between them, those put an event’s details, its map and its schedule in an attendee’s pocket, and let the organiser keep them up to date.

The activities and stalls screens in those images use the details of a real festival, The Slipshod Gathering, which agreed to us setting up a demo to show what the app could do. The home screen lists it alongside a second event.

My part

I built the Flutter mobile app. It’s the part attendees hold in their hands: the listings, the map, the schedule, the notifications and the QR code scanning described above. Flutter is a framework for building mobile apps, and Dart is the language you write them in. I also helped build the Laravel backend with the CEO. Digitwell was just the two of us at the time.

What was hard

It was my first Flutter app. I had to learn Flutter and Dart from scratch while building the real app to a timeline, so the learning and the delivering happened at the same time.

Learning from a tutorial is one thing. Learning while a real app has to work, and be ready on time, is another.

How it ended up

Find Your Event never got the funding it needed to reach the market, so it didn’t get the marketing push to put it in front of events. It’s still on the Play Store.

For me, it’s where I learned Flutter properly, under real deadlines. That fits what’s on my CV for Digitwell: learning Flutter and Dart from scratch, and leading the adoption of Flutter to replace a long-standing legacy platform.

It was all built before AI tools. Looking back, it’s a useful marker for how differently I work now.
