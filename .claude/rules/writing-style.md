# Writing style

The site is in Brad's voice. It should read like a thoughtful colleague explaining their work, not a brochure.

## Voice

- **British English.** organise, behaviour, colour, programme (for a TV show; "program" for software), licence (noun), catalogue, whilst is fine but "while" is simpler. Dates as "22 October 2026".
- **First person.** "I" for Brad's own actions, "we" for the team (see `honesty-and-credit.md`). No third-person bio voice ("Brad is a passionate...").
- **Plain and specific.** Say what happened, to what, and why it mattered. Name the technology at a general level.
- **Concrete over abstract.** An example beats an adjective. If a sentence could appear on any developer's site, rewrite it.
- **Short sentences.** Aim for under 25 words. One idea each. Short paragraphs of 1 to 4 sentences.
- **Active voice** unless the actor does not matter.
- **Warm, not salesy.** Curious and a little dry is fine. Enthusiasm shown through detail, not exclamation marks.

## Avoid

- Buzzwords: leverage, synergy, ecosystem (unless literal), robust, scalable (unless you say what scaled), passionate, results-driven, thought leader, innovative, solutions.
- Hype (see `honesty-and-credit.md`).
- Filler openers: "In today's fast-moving world", "It's worth noting that".
- Em dashes as a habit. Use at most one in a paragraph, and prefer a full stop, comma or colon. Do not use them as a stylistic tic.
- Stacks of three adjectives or a rhythm of triplets in every paragraph.
- Rhetorical questions as headings.
- Emoji.
- Exclamation marks.

## Structure

- **Headings in sentence case**: "What was hard", not "What Was Hard".
- One `h1` per page. Do not skip heading levels.
- Lead with the point. Put the headline fact in the first sentence of a section.
- Link text says where the link goes ("read the case study"), never "click here".
- Lists for parallel items only. Do not break reasoning into bullet fragments.
- Alt text describes what the image shows and why it is there, in a plain sentence. Decorative images get empty alt. Alt text is subject to `confidentiality.md`.
- Spell out acronyms on first use unless they are universal (HTML, CSS, PDF).

## Before and after

**These examples are illustrative. Their facts are invented. Never reuse them as site content**; write from what Brad has confirmed.

| Before | After |
| --- | --- |
| "I'm a passionate developer who leverages cutting-edge AI to deliver robust solutions." | "I write software and work on a team that builds with AI agents. Most of my day is reviewing and fixing what the agents get wrong." |
| "It was an incredibly challenging yet rewarding journey!" | "The hard part was keeping agents on task. We ended up checking their work more closely." |
| "Case Study: The Platform Build" | "Case study: building an internal platform with AI agents" |
| "Click here to see more." | "Read the full case study." |

## Length

Case studies: 600 to 1,200 words. Landing page sections: a short paragraph plus a link. If a section runs long, cut the weakest sentence first, then the second weakest.

Reading aloud is a good test. If you would not say it to a colleague, change it.
